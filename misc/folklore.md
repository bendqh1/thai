# Thai Folklore: Buildings, Spirits, and Cultural Notes

## Thai Folklore Buildings

| Thai Name | English / Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ศาลพระภูมิ | San Phra Phum / spirit house | A small shrine associated with พระภูมิ, a guardian spirit believed to protect a property or place. |
| ศาลเจ้าที่ | San Jhao Thi / shrine for the land spirits | A shrine associated with เจ้าผีเจ้าที่, spirits believed to inhabit and protect a particular place or piece of land. |
| แท่นวางศาลพระภูมิ | Platform/base for a spirit house | A raised structure used to support a ศาลพระภูมิ. |

## Thai Folklore Spirits Associated with Protection or Good Fortune

| Thai Name | English / Description | Cultural Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ผีตะเคียน / เจ้าแม่ตะเคียน | Takian tree spirit / Lady Takian | A female tree spirit associated with the ตะเคียน tree. Traditional beliefs about spirits inhabiting old trees can contribute to reluctance to cut down large, old trees. |
| กุมาร / กุมารทอง | Kuman / Kuman Thong | A supernatural child spirit traditionally associated with protection, luck, or other supernatural assistance. Not necessarily evil; beliefs vary. |

## Thai Spirits Associated with Misfortune and Danger

| Thai Name | English / Description | Traditional Association / Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ผีเปรต | Preta / hungry ghost | A spirit suffering the consequences of wrongdoing. Stories can function as moral teachings. The exact duration of punishment varies by tradition; 100–500 years should not be treated as a universal rule. |
| เสือสมิง | Weretiger / tiger spirit | A supernatural being associated with a tiger, often portrayed as dangerous or malevolent. |
| ผีตานี | Nang Tani / Tani spirit | A female spirit associated with the banana tree, commonly depicted as a beautiful woman wearing green. |
| ผีกระหัง | Krahang / male flying spirit | A male supernatural being traditionally depicted flying at night using rice-winnowing baskets as wings. It is the male counterpart to ผีกระสือ. |
| ผีพราย | Phrai / water-associated ghost | A ghost or spirit associated with water, drowning, and death in or near water. |
| ผีถ้วยแก้ว | Glass-cup spirit / séance spirit | Associated with the ผีถ้วยแก้ว séance practice, in which a glass or cup is used as a supposed means of communicating with spirits. |
| ผีปอบ | Pob / possessing spirit | A malevolent spirit traditionally believed to possess people and consume their internal organs or bodily substances. |
| ผีตายโหง | Ghost of an unnatural or sudden death | A ghost associated with an unnatural, sudden, or violent death. |

## Thai Folklore Spirits Associated with Love

| Thai Name | English / Description | Cultural Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ผีแม่นาค | Mae Nak | One of Thailand's most famous ghost legends, centered on a woman whose love and attachment to her husband continue after death. |

## Cultural Notes

| Notes |
|---|
| Many if not most of the Thai ghosts are female |
| Female ghosts are often portrayed as particularly frightening in Thai folklore. |
| Many Thai supernatural stories have a moral or cautionary function, such as stories about ผีเปรต emphasizing the consequences of wrongdoing. |
| ตัวตายตัวแทน or “Death replacement” is related folklore belief in which another person becomes the victim of a supernatural force or haunting after someone dies. |
